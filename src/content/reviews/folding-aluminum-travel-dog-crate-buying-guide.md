The listing describes a folding aluminum crate for medium and large dogs, intended for vehicle travel or camping. “Travel crate” does not prove airline acceptance or crash protection. The buyer must verify the exact dimensions, construction, locks, and the rules of the journey.

## Match the crate to the dog

Measure standing height from floor to the top of the head or ears, plus length from nose to tail and the pet’s width at the shoulders. Compare these figures with the internal usable dimensions, not external frame size. The source does not publish the inner measurements or a weight limit.

Check door placement, side openings, latch type, floor base, ventilation, and whether the selected size folds to a workable transport shape. Ask how the aluminum panels, frame joints, and locks are constructed. A medium-or-large label is too broad to establish fit.

## Vehicle and transport fit

Measure the cargo or back-seat space, including seat angle and available floor clearance. Confirm whether anchors or straps are included and identify every rated attachment point. The listing provides no vehicle-specific fit, anchoring kit, or crash-test result.

For airline travel, do not rely on the word “approved” unless the exact model is documented by the relevant carrier and authority. Rules can differ by carrier, route, aircraft, and itinerary. Ask the carrier separately and verify the crate close to departure.

## Assembly and setup

Unfold the crate on a level surface and engage every frame lock. Test both doors by hand, check that bars or panels cannot pinch, and confirm that ventilation remains open. Recheck fasteners and locking points after the first fold-and-unfold cycle.

For a stationary practice period, place familiar bedding outside the door without blocking airflow. Introduce the dog slowly and supervise. Never leave a pet unattended in a hot parked vehicle or in a crate that may shift.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing the crate from the dog’s breed or a broad size label.
- Assuming external dimensions equal usable internal space.
- Treating aluminum construction as a verified crash standard.
- Using a vehicle anchor or strap not specified for the crate.
- Folding or storing the crate while a latch or panel is misaligned.
